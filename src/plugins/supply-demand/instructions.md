# Supply and Demand

Explore how buyers and sellers interact in a market. The supply curve slopes upward, the demand curve slopes downward, and their intersection is the market equilibrium.

## Mark a market point

Use `setPrice` and `setQuantity` to choose a point, then call `draw` to place it on the graph.

```javascript
setPrice(1.25);
setQuantity(0.9);
draw();
```

- **Price** belongs on the vertical axis.
- **Quantity** belongs on the horizontal axis.
- Calling `draw()` uses the most recently set price and quantity.

Try drawing multiple points to compare different market situations.

## Supply and demand curves

- **Supply** slopes upward because producers are generally willing to sell more at higher prices.
- **Demand** slopes downward because consumers generally purchase less at higher prices.
- A rightward shift represents an increase in supply or demand.
- A leftward shift represents a decrease in supply or demand.

The solid lines show the original curves. Shifted curves appear as dashed lines so you can compare the new market with the starting market.

## Equilibrium

Equilibrium occurs where quantity supplied equals quantity demanded. The equilibrium point determines both the market price and market quantity.

When the equilibrium helper is enabled, dashed guide lines connect the equilibrium point to both axes. The statistics below the graph report its price and quantity.

## Explore

Change the starter values and run the code again. Consider:

- What happens when price is above equilibrium?
- What happens when price is below equilibrium?
- How would increased production costs shift supply?
- How would an increase in consumer income shift demand for a normal good?
- Which shift changes both equilibrium price and equilibrium quantity?
